Sofia Iqbal

Co-founder · Spark & Spectrum CIC

"Every person carries something extraordinary. My job is to help them see it."

Sofia co-founded Spark & Spectrum with a clear purpose: to make affirming, human-first support available to individuals and families who often feel unseen. With a background in emotional intelligence, SEND and youth work, she brings warmth, honesty and deep knowledge of neurodivergent experience to every session.

Sofia delivers programmes face to face across the Midlands and remotely by video. She works with individuals, parents, young people and groups.

Selina Afzal

Co-founder · Spark & Spectrum CIC

"Understanding how you work is the beginning of everything. I walk that journey with you."

Selina co-founded Spark & Spectrum to bring neurodiversity-affirming practice into communities that are often left behind by mainstream services. She works with individuals and families navigating neurodivergence, special needs and the complex terrain of raising a child who experiences the world differently.

Selina delivers face to face across the Midlands and remotely, with particular experience supporting families in faith and community settings.

What we offer

Support for every kind of journey

Understanding Your Spark

A self-discovery journey for neurodivergent individuals. Five areas of gentle, affirming exploration at your own pace.

Becoming the Parent They Need

Twelve reflective sessions for parents raising a neurodivergent, special needs or medical needs child.

Managing Your Spark

Follow-on support for those who have completed Understanding Your Spark. Practical strategies for everyday life.

Growing With Your Spark

For neurodivergent young people in transition. Identity, resilience and self-belief through change.

Vision Loss & Medical Needs

Affirming support for individuals and families navigating vision loss, developmental differences or complex medical needs.

When Home Feels Like a Storm

For families impacted by neurodivergent or special needs behaviour at home. Meltdowns, demand avoidance, siblings and parental wellbeing.
